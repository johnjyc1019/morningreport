## Macro Backdrop

Markets are navigating a confluence of tightening monetary policy, elevated energy prices, and ongoing Middle East geopolitical risk. The Federal Reserve hiked rates **25bps on September 16**, bringing the overnight lending rate to **3.75%–4.00%** — the first hike in three years — with the FOMC signaling another potential move in December contingent on inflation data. Chairman Warsh highlighted that "inflation remains elevated" and the hike will "support a timelier return to the 2% goal." The **10-year Treasury yield climbed back above 5.0%** post-decision, a level not seen in nearly two decades, driven by sticky core CPI (August print came in above consensus) and surging oil prices. **Brent crude hovers near $99/bbl** amid Middle East conflict that has disrupted Saudi pipeline flows and raised concerns about Strait of Hormuz transit risk. Adding to the global tightening backdrop, the **Bank of Japan raised its policy rate 25bps to 1.25% this morning** (7-2 vote), the highest rate since 1995 and the fastest pace of hikes since normalization began in March 2024.
