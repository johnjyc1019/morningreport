## Macro Backdrop

US equities closed Friday on a positive note, with the S&P 500 +0.4%, Nasdaq 100 +0.3%, and the Dow adding 518 points, following a report showing US business activity expanding at its fastest pace in more than four years. The week ahead is heavy on macro and earnings risk: the NVDA print Wednesday will dominate sentiment.

**Bond market stress persists.** The 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.311% — highest since June 2007 — driven by oil-led inflation fears and fiscal concerns. The 10-year sits at ~4.724%. Oil surged ~5% last week (WTI $82.13, Brent $87.72) on renewed Strait of Hormuz shipping disruptions as Iran/Middle East tensions flared. Rate-hike probability for September FOMC has eased to ~52% (from 67% a week ago) after softer payrolls, but oil-driven CPI could reprice that quickly.

**July CPI estimate:** 3.4% YoY (vs. 3.5% prior); Core CPI 2.5% YoY (vs. 2.6%).

**Geopolitical risk:** The Iran-US/Israel conflict continues to restrict Hormuz tanker traffic; the US-China trade war has broadened beyond initial bilateral tariffs to include additional sectors and partners; European political fragmentation adds a further uncertainty premium.
