## Macro Backdrop

Global markets are navigating the aftermath of a severe AI-trade meltdown that struck Thursday, November 5. The S&P 500 fell ~4.4%, the Nasdaq dropped ~5–6.5%, and the Dow shed ~3.4% in one of the worst single-session routs in recent memory — erasing roughly $2.5 trillion in Nasdaq market cap. The catalyst: a sharp reassessment of AI infrastructure spending expectations, amplified by fears that a Democratic-controlled Congress will constrain tech-sector investment.

Overlay on that backdrop: the ongoing US-Iran conflict continues to stoke energy inflation, with Brent crude trading near $99/bbl. The Fed hiked rates in September (now at 3.75–4.00%) in response to oil-driven re-acceleration in inflation, and markets are pricing approximately 83% odds of a further 25bps hike next month. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to ~4.83% from a recent high above 5.10% as investors fled to safety.

Overnight, US equity futures staged a partial recovery — Nasdaq futures turned positive, S&P 500 futures roughly flat — as dip buyers stepped in and Asia's sharp losses attracted contrarian interest.

## Trade Ideas

1. **Defensives rotation (overweight utilities, healthcare, staples):** Thursday's selloff lifted insurance, financial services, and healthcare to record highs while tech hit 52-week lows. If AI spending derating continues, this rotation has further to run.

2. **Short duration play:** With the Fed still in hiking mode and oil sticky near $99/bbl, the recent Treasury rally to 4.83% may be a tactical safe-haven bounce rather than a durable move lower. Consider reducing long-duration exposure on any further yield compression.

3. **Toyota (TM) — post-earnings dip buyer:** The +17% move on a solid beat and raised guidance is real alpha, but watch for a pullback toward the gap. Toyota benefits from non-AI, non-tech exposure and strong pricing power. Near-term support ~$215.

4. **Energy sector (XLE/OIH):** US-Iran conflict continues to underpin a Brent crude floor near $95. Any ceasefire risk would be a negative catalyst, but absent that, energy names with operational leverage remain well-bid.
