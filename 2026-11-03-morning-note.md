## Macro Backdrop

Markets are navigating a tricky confluence of macro pressures heading into Tuesday's US midterm elections. Oil prices remain the primary driver of cross-asset volatility, with Brent crude oscillating around $104 as Iran conflict diplomacy moves in fits and starts — Qatar-mediated Strait of Hormuz talks briefly sent Brent below $100 before fresh Gulf shipping attacks pushed it back up. The Federal Reserve's September hike (to 3.75%–4.00%) has tightened financial conditions meaningfully, with the 10-year Treasury yield hovering near 4.92% and CPI last printing at 3.7% YoY in September. The next CPI release (October data) is due November 10. Market consensus is pricing another 25 bps hike in December. The S&P 500 is down roughly 10% from its recent high, with Nasdaq 100 in official correction territory, pressured by an ongoing chipmaker selloff.

**US midterm elections today** add a layer of uncertainty — historically a source of short-term volatility before markets refocus on fundamentals.

## Trade Ideas

1. **Long Marriott (MAR) on RevPAR upgrade** — Global travel demand holding up despite macro headwinds; raised guidance is a catalyst. Risk: consumer slowdown if Fed overtightens.

2. **Avoid Intel (INTC) near-term** — A 29% single-day decline suggests deep structural concerns beyond one quarter; do not catch the falling knife until guidance stabilizes. Watch for insider buying as an eventual signal.

3. **Oil Vol Hedge** — With Brent oscillating $10+ on headlines, consider long straddles on oil ETFs (USO, OIL). A Hormuz reopening could collapse Brent toward $85; a deal collapse could spike above $115.

4. **China tech / Hong Kong dip** — Hang Seng futures up 0.7% on state support measures. If PBOC action proves durable, Alibaba/Tencent ADRs offer asymmetric upside from depressed levels. Catalyst: confirmation of state purchases.

5. **Post-midterm rotation** — Historically, the S&P 500 rallies in the 3–6 months following midterms regardless of outcome. A divided Congress (likely outcome) has historically been bullish for equities. Consider adding broad market exposure on any election-day dip.

### News (Last 24 Hours)
- **New CFO:** POET named **Giovanni Pacelli** as Chief Financial Officer (announced Nov 2). Thomas Mika, the long-tenured CFO, had previously announced retirement. Leadership transition adds near-term uncertainty.
- No additional SEC filings or press releases confirmed as of this writing.

### Key Risk Status

| Risk | Status |
|---|---|
| **Marvell / Celestial AI order cancellation & NDA lawsuit** | **Active.** In April 2026, Marvell (which acquired Celestial AI) cancelled all Celestial AI purchase orders, citing POET's alleged breach of confidentiality obligations by disclosing order/shipping details. POET stock fell 46% on the news before rebounding 34.6% by May 7. No resolution announced; litigation risk remains. |
| **Securities class actions (PFIC tax misrepresentation)** | **Active.** Multiple federal securities class actions filed in 2026, alleging misleading statements about POET's PFIC (Passive Foreign Investment Company) tax status and confidentiality obligations. Class period and lead plaintiff determinations pending. |
| **$400M capital raise dilution** | **Ongoing overhang.** May 2026 registered direct offering: ~19.05M shares at $21.00 + warrants for another 19.05M shares (exercise price ~$26.15–$26.25, 3-year term), all to a single unnamed institutional investor. The raise exceeded POET's then-market cap (~$380M). Stock has since collapsed from the $21 offering price to ~$4.80 — ~77% below offer price. Dilution and execution risk weigh heavily. |

### Outlook: **BEARISH / HIGH RISK**
POET is in a difficult multi-front situation: core customer relationship (Marvell/Celestial AI) severed, securities litigation ongoing, leadership in transition, and the stock trading at a fraction of its May 2026 capital-raise price. Near-term catalyst is Q3 earnings on Nov 16 — revenue vs. the ~$500K Q1 print and any Lumilens JDA order updates will be critical. The $50M Lumilens purchase order and alternate ~$5M customer order are the only visible revenue supports. Do not size aggressively unless litigation risk clears and Lumilens ramp materializes in reported numbers.
